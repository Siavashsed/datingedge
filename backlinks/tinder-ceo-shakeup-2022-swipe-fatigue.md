## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why are so many people getting burned out on swiping apps like Tinder?

As a clinical psychologist who works with attachment and modern relationships, I hear this often. In my view the burnout comes from the mechanics of the swipe, not from dating itself.

First, swiping reduces people to a card and a split-second judgment. When you make that judgment dozens of times in one sitting, your brain starts to treat profiles as items to sort rather than humans to meet. That makes the whole experience feel mechanical and emotionally flat.

Second, the reward schedule is unpredictable. An occasional match works like a slot machine payout, which keeps you checking but rarely leaves you satisfied. Many people tell me they open the app out of habit, not out of hope.

Third, there is the cost to self-worth. Every unanswered like or stalled conversation can register as small rejection. People with anxious attachment tend to feel this most, because they read silence as a verdict on themselves. Avoidant styles can get a different trap, since endless options make it easy to keep everyone at arm's length.

Fourth, choice overload makes commitment harder. When someone else always seems one swipe away, it gets harder to invest in the person in front of you.

A few things help my clients. Set a time limit and stop when it's up. Move promising matches to a call or a coffee quickly instead of letting conversations drift. Take deliberate breaks without guilt. And notice how you feel after using the app, not only what you got from it.
