## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does someone become more attractive when they're less available or harder to reach?

This is one of the most common patterns I see in my clinical practice, and it has genuine psychological roots.

When someone's availability is unpredictable or limited, several things happen simultaneously in the brain. First, uncertainty triggers dopamine release—the same neurochemical involved in reward processing. Your brain becomes more engaged when outcomes aren't guaranteed. This is why variable rewards (like social media notifications or intermittent responses) create stronger engagement than consistent ones.

Second, scarcity creates perceived value. When something—or someone—is harder to access, our brain perceives them as more valuable. This isn't a flaw in our psychology; it's an adaptive mechanism that evolved to help us prioritize limited resources.

Third, delayed availability creates space for imagination and projection. When someone isn't constantly available, you fill in the gaps with your own narrative about who they are. This projection often feels more attractive than the reality.

Here's the crucial distinction: there's a difference between genuine scarcity (someone actually has a full life, boundaries, and priorities) versus manufactured games (strategic unavailability designed to manipulate). One builds sustainable attraction; the other creates anxiety.

Understanding the actual psychology—how reward systems, uncertainty, and perceived value work together—helps you distinguish between healthy attraction patterns and unhealthy attachment dynamics.
