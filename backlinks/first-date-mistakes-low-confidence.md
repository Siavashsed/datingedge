## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What first date mistakes make someone look insecure or low in confidence?

As a clinical psychologist who works with attachment and modern relationships, I'd push back gently on the common answer, which is "being visibly nervous." In my experience, nerves are normal and often endearing. What reads as insecurity is something different: managing.

There are three patterns I see most.

1. Scripting. Some clients prepare a list of questions or stories and run through them in order. The problem isn't preparation itself. It's that when you're executing a plan, you can't listen. The other person senses they're being interviewed rather than met.

2. Apologizing for yourself. Lines like "sorry, I'm rambling" or "I'm probably not that interesting" feel like humility, but they quietly ask the other person to reassure you. That shifts the date from connection to emotional caretaking.

3. Scanning for a verdict. Checking their face every minute or so to see whether it's going well means your attention is on your own evaluation, not on them. People feel that split attention even if they can't name it.

All three come from the same root: an attempt to control how you're perceived. Secure people aren't free of nerves. They're simply more willing to tolerate uncertainty, let a pause sit, and be seen without a pre-approved performance.

A few practical shifts help. Pick one or two topics you're genuinely curious about instead of a list. Replace an apology with nothing at all. And when you notice yourself scanning their face, bring your attention back to what they're actually saying.
