## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why am I always attracted to the same type of person, even when it never works out?

Because what you're drawn to usually isn't a type at all. It's a feeling you already know.

In my practice, people often describe their type in surface terms: tall, creative, ambitious, a certain sense of humor. When we look closer, those traits vary far more than they expected. What stays the same is how it feels to be with that person: anxious and hopeful, needing to earn attention, feeling most alive when the other person is slightly out of reach.

That pattern tends to go back to childhood. The way love worked in the home you grew up in becomes your nervous system's reference point for closeness. Maybe it was steady, conditional, unpredictable or distant. As adults we read that familiar feeling as chemistry. A partner who recreates it feels exciting. A partner who is calm and consistent can feel strangely flat. Nothing is wrong with them. There's just nothing to recognize.

This is a core idea in attachment research, and it explains why people repeat patterns they consciously want to escape. I wrote a longer piece on it, including a client whose eleven exes all shared one thing she had never tracked: https://siavashsed.github.io/datingedge/childhood-family-dynamics-shape-attraction/

A few things that help in practice:

1. Track the feeling, not the traits. After a first date, ask yourself what your body felt, not what they do for work.
2. Treat intense early chemistry as information, not a verdict.
3. Give steady people a few more dates than your instincts want to.
4. If the pattern is painful or keeps repeating, an attachment-focused therapist can help you build a new reference point.

Your type was learned, which means it can be relearned.
