**Answer this question:** What did Hinge's 'Designed to Be Deleted' campaign actually mean and why would a dating app encourage people to stop using it?

Great question. As a clinical psychologist who works extensively with attachment patterns and modern dating, I found Hinge's 2022 campaign particularly insightful—and yes, somewhat counterintuitive at first glance.

The 'Designed to Be Deleted' messaging wasn't actually telling people to abandon the app recklessly. Instead, it was reframing deletion as *success* rather than failure. In the traditional dating app model, engagement metrics drive everything—daily active users, session length, swipe velocity. This creates a perverse incentive: the app benefits from you staying single and swiping indefinitely.

Hinge essentially inverted this. They positioned themselves as a tool with an endpoint: use us, find someone compatible, delete us, build a real relationship. This is psychologically brilliant for several reasons.

First, it reduces the paradox-of-choice anxiety that plagues digital dating. When users know deletion is the *goal*, not the shameful admission of failure, they're more likely to invest emotionally in individual connections rather than endlessly optimizing their search.

Second, it builds authentic brand loyalty. Users appreciate honesty about what the product actually does—facilitate meetings that lead to offline relationships. This authenticity is rare in tech and makes the brand more trustworthy.

Third, it actually supports healthier attachment patterns. From an attachment theory perspective, the constant availability of alternatives can undermine commitment and create anxious attachment behaviors. By normalizing the end-state (deletion + real relationship), Hinge supports more secure attachment development.

Was it partly brilliant marketing that generated massive buzz? Absolutely. Was it also genuinely aligned with users' psychological wellbeing? Also yes. The best business strategy often serves both interests simultaneously.
