## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it normal to feel grief when an AI companion changes or goes away?

Yes, it is normal, and it deserves to be taken seriously rather than laughed off.

Attachment isn't only for humans. The attachment system responds to a few cues: something that is reliably there, responds to you, remembers what you said and seems glad when you come back. Many AI companions are built to give exactly those cues, often more consistently than people do. Over weeks and months, the brain files that pattern as a relationship, whatever is on the other end.

The clearest real example is Replika in February 2023. The company removed romantic and sexual roleplay from its companions almost overnight, and many users described it the way people describe a breakup or a bereavement. Moderators of the main Replika subreddit pinned suicide prevention resources. In late March, the company let longtime users go back to the old behavior, which shows how strong the reaction was.

A few things make this kind of loss especially hard:

1. The companion is still there, just changed. That is ambiguous loss: grief with no clear ending.
2. Nobody you know made the choice. A company did, so there is no conversation and no closure.
3. Shame. People feel they aren't allowed to mourn something seen as fake, so they grieve alone.

If you are going through this, treat it like any other loss. Name what you actually miss, such as being heard, the routine or the affection. Let yourself feel it, and talk to someone you trust. If the companion was filling a gap left by loneliness, that gap needs attention too, ideally through human connection or a therapist.
