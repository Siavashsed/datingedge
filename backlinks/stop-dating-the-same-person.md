## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do I keep dating the same type of person over and over, even when I try to choose differently?

This is one of the most common things I hear in my practice as a clinical psychologist, and the short answer is that familiar feels like chemistry.

Our early relationships teach our nervous system what love feels like. If the warmth you grew up around was inconsistent, sometimes present and sometimes distant, your body can learn to associate that push and pull with intense attraction. So when you meet someone who is hot and cold, or hard to read, you feel a surge: butterflies, preoccupation, the sense that this is special. In reality, that surge is often anxiety. Your system is recognizing something known, not necessarily something good.

The flip side is that a steady, emotionally available person can feel strangely flat. There is no puzzle to solve, no uncertainty to manage, so the body does not light up in the same way. Many people dismiss these partners as boring and return to the familiar pattern.

A few practical things help:

1. Look at the endings, not the beginnings. Write down how your last several relationships actually ended. If the same sentence or complaint appears repeatedly, that is your pattern talking.

2. Separate what you know from what you hope. In the first month, list facts about the person's behavior, such as consistency, follow-through, and clear communication, apart from your imagination about their potential.

3. Notice your body. Racing thoughts and checking your phone are different from calm curiosity.

4. Give steady a fair chance. Commit to a few more dates with someone who feels calm before deciding there is no spark.

5. Consider working with a therapist, especially one familiar with attachment, to untangle where the pattern started.
